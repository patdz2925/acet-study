Compound interest calculates growth where accumulated interest also earns interest.

Formulas:
1. Compounded n times per year:
   A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)
   Where: P = Principal, r = annual rate (decimal), n = compounding frequency, t = years.
   - Annually: n = 1
   - Semi-annually: n = 2
   - Quarterly: n = 4
   - Monthly: n = 12
2. Simple Interest (for comparison):
   I = P × r × t;  A = P + I = P(1 + rt).

Example: $1,000 invested at 10% compounded semi-annually for 1 year.
  A = 1000(1 + 0.10/2)^(2 × 1) = 1000(1.05)² = 1000(1.1025) = $1,102.50.
  (Simple interest would yield only $1,100).

Tip: In ACET, problems usually use simple periods (1 or 2 years) or small integers. Watch out for the compounding period!
