Simplifying, adding, multiplying, and rationalizing radicals (surds).

Key Rules:
1. Product of Radicals: √(a × b) = √a × √b (for a, b ≥ 0).
2. Quotient of Radicals: √(a / b) = √a / √b.
3. Simplifying: Pull out perfect square factors:
   √72 = √(36 × 2) = 6√2.
4. Adding/Subtracting: Only LIKE radicals can be combined:
   3√5 + 2√5 = 5√5. (Cannot combine √2 + √3!).
5. Rationalizing the Denominator:
   - Single term: 1 / √a = √a / a.
   - Conjugate pair: 1 / (√a + √b) = (√a - √b) / (a - b).

Example: Simplify 1 / (3 - √2)
  Multiply numerator and denominator by conjugate (3 + √2):
  (3 + √2) / [(3 - √2)(3 + √2)] = (3 + √2) / (3² - 2) = (3 + √2) / 7.

Tip: Never leave a radical in the denominator of a final fraction choice.
