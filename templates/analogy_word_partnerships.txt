Word Partnerships (Collocations & Idioms) examine words that naturally pair together to form established meanings.

Relationship: [WORD A] and [WORD B] form a standard phrase, compound, or complementary pair.

Strategy:
1. Formulate how the two words interact in common usage.
2. Consider complementary pairs: Lock and Key, Salt and Pepper, Bread and Butter.
3. Check for dependent associations where one word requires the other to fulfill its function.

Example: LOCK : KEY
  Bridge: A lock requires a key to open and function.
  Match: NEEDLE : THREAD (A needle requires thread to fulfill its purpose in sewing).

Tip: Ensure the relationship is reciprocal or mutually purposeful, not just random association.
