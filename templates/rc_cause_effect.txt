Cause and Effect questions test the ability to recognize causal relationships in texts.

Key Indicators & Strategy:
1. Look for explicit causal markers: because, as a result, consequently, due to, led to, produced by, thereby.
2. Distinguish between Cause and Correlation: Just because Event B followed Event A does not necessarily mean Event A caused Event B.
3. Identify multi-step causal chains: Factor A -> Factor B -> Outcome C.
4. Don't invert the relationship: Make sure you identify what caused what (Cause vs. Effect).

Example: "Increased ocean temperatures led to coral bleaching, which drastically reduced fish biodiversity."
  Primary Cause: Higher ocean temperatures.
  Immediate Effect: Coral bleaching.
  Subsequent Effect: Decline in fish biodiversity.

Tip: Scan the passage specifically for the keyword mentioned in the question stem and read the sentences immediately before and after it.
