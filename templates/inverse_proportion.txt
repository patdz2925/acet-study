Inverse proportion describes a relationship where an increase in one variable causes a proportional decrease in another.

Core Concept:
  x · y = k (constant product)
  Or: x₁ · y₁ = x₂ · y₂

Common Real-World Contexts:
- Workers and time to complete a job (more workers = fewer days).
- Speed and time for a fixed distance (faster speed = less travel time).
- Pressure and volume of a gas (Boyle's Law).

Example: If 6 workers can build a wall in 10 days, how many days will 15 workers take at the same rate?
  x₁y₁ = x₂y₂
  6 × 10 = 15 × d
  60 = 15d  =>  d = 4 days.

Tip: In direct proportion, the RATIO (y/x) is constant. In inverse proportion, the PRODUCT (x · y) is constant!
