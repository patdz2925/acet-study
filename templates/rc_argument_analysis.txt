Argument Analysis evaluates your ability to dissect claims, premises, and logical conclusions.

Key Concepts & Strategy:
1. Identify the Main Conclusion: What is the author ultimately trying to prove or persuade you to accept?
2. Distinguish Premises from Claims: Premises are stated facts or evidence; conclusions are inferences derived from them.
3. Check for Assumptions: An unstated premise necessary for the argument to hold true.
4. Identify Flaws: Common logical fallacies include hasty generalization, circular reasoning, and correlation vs. causation.

Example: If an argument claims "City X reduced crime after adding streetlights; therefore, streetlights reduce crime," it assumes no other variables (like increased police patrols) caused the reduction.

Tip: The correct answer strictly follows the given text. Do not rely on outside knowledge or personal opinions.
