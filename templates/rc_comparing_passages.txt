Comparing Passages evaluates your ability to synthesize information across two texts (Passage 1 and Passage 2).

Key Strategy:
1. Read Passage 1 first: Note its main idea, purpose, and author's tone.
2. Read Passage 2: Identify where it agrees with, expands upon, or directly opposes Passage 1.
3. Common Relationships:
   - Complementary: Passage 2 provides specific evidence or a case study supporting Passage 1.
   - Opposing: Passage 2 refutes or presents a counter-argument to Passage 1's thesis.
   - General vs. Specific: Passage 1 offers a broad overview; Passage 2 delves into a specific instance.
4. When asked how Author 2 would respond to Author 1, cite specific lines from Passage 2 reflecting their stance.

Tip: Eliminate choices that are true for only one passage when the question asks about both.
