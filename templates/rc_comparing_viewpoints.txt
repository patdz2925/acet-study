Comparing Viewpoints examines differing perspectives or interpretations within one or more passages.

Key Strategy:
1. Attribute every argument to its speaker: Who holds Viewpoint A (e.g., traditional economists) vs. Viewpoint B (e.g., behavioral economists)?
2. Identify the exact point of disagreement (the crux of the controversy).
3. Look for concessions: Does one viewpoint acknowledge valid points made by the other?
4. Determine the author's stance: Does the author endorse Viewpoint A, endorse Viewpoint B, synthesize both, or remain completely neutral?

Example: If Viewpoint 1 argues that AI boosts human productivity while Viewpoint 2 argues that AI displaces workers, the core debate centers on the net impact of automation on employment.

Tip: Never confuse a reported viewpoint ("Critics claim that...") with the author's own perspective.
